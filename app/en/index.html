Put the fact back.

Sometimes a sentence begins with a situation and quietly turns into a verdict about you. This tool does not reassure you or write the “right answer.” It only helps you notice the shift and write down what actually happened.

01 / 03

Write the exact sentence in your head.

Do not make it reasonable first. Do not clean it up.

02 / 03

Write down the event.

Only include what could be seen, heard, counted, or checked. Do not explain why yet, and do not evaluate yourself.

Not: “I wasted another day because I am lazy.”
Try: “I planned to work on the video today, but I did not start.”

03 / 03

Same event. Two sentences.

WHAT YOU SAID

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

Both sentences refer to the same event. Only one of them leaves room for a next step.

THIS IS A FEELING

The feeling does not need correcting.

This sentence describes your present experience without turning it into a verdict about who you are. If a harsher sentence follows it, write that one instead.

SAFETY FIRST

This tool is not the right tool for this moment.

If you may act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, contact local emergency services now or reach a person who can stay with you. Do not remain alone.
